On-Screen Hooks — Reference

The other bank. `winning-hooks.md` is VERBAL hooks: the sentence that is spoken
over the first seconds. These are ON-SCREEN hooks: the text card that sits on
the frame. A video has both, and they are not interchangeable -- a verbal hook
is a full sentence, an on-screen hook is three to eight words.

Same rule as the verbal bank: match, never write. Take the source that already
worked and change as few words as possible. Do NOT add, remove, or modify the
source list -- these are hooks that ran.

Supplied by the operator's mentor's dashboard, 45 worked examples across five
niches. Recorded exactly as generated, including which source each was matched
from and what was changed, because the accounting is the part worth copying.

== What the examples teach ==

Read down the "changed" lines and the method is visible:

- The swap is usually ONE noun. 'Marketing Agency' -> 'videoredigerare',
  'clawbot setup' -> 'AI-systemet', 'the business' -> 'hemsidan'. The
  structure around it is untouched.
- Punctuation is structure, not decoration. The trailing '...' and the
  QUOTED CAPS are carried over verbatim; they are what makes the card read
  as a confession or an overheard objection rather than a headline.
- A quoted-caps source becomes a quoted-caps card. A lowercase source stays
  lowercase.
- Word-for-word is a legitimate and common outcome. Four of the 45 change
  nothing but the language.
- The same source is reused across niches -- 'Replaced My Marketing Agency'
  five times, 'WRONG THING' four. Reuse is the point of a bank.
